# Agency programme closeout (SSL-852)

This implements the remaining web loop from Liam's 17 November decisions. The later
weekly passport decision (SSL-849) remains the main research view. Project totals
explain contributions; they are not separate project goals or extra passport stamps.

- The programme describes owned craft, projects, actual personal data per day and
  contributions. Ground scope levels and finite probe coverage are respected.
- Orbital instrument launches remain available. Each completed sortie has one launch
  receipt; old ownership becomes one legacy craft. Launching another craft adds daily
  capacity, rather than silently increasing the first telescope's level.
- Each launch ages independently. Servicing restores the fleet of that instrument
  type. The desk reports the lowest condition; a fresh craft still produces data when
  an older craft has failed. Ground scopes and probes retain their existing rules.
- Spare orbital craft can feed the shared queue instead of the personal feed. The
  first craft remains personal. Donating is an allocation, not a contribution or stamp;
  another agency still needs to classify the data. The shared pool adds at most 20
  daily items per project to a recipient's feed, bounded by the available real records.
- Daily item allowances are consumed by today’s completed work; classifying an item does not endlessly refill the window. Older classifications do not consume today’s data.
- Classifications identify the project on the daily stamp and Garden ledger row.
  There is still one stamp of a kind per day; all project records remain in the fleet
  totals. Training records are excluded from public science totals.
- Community research links out to instruments. Its programme reference shows shared
  contributions, donated capacity, shared probe sectors and verified worlds alongside
  existing independent consensus reviews. No instrument tool was moved into research.
- The community projection reads synced saves, not the pending local outbox. Probe
  readings are unioned by body/sector. Shared sectors can inform a run but never grant
  the recipient a probe-observation stamp; their own probe must download fresh data.
- Independent positive TESS consensus publishes the corresponding saved exoplanet
  target to a permanent, server-only registry. It joins everyone's normal star map and
  mission targets. A single personal verdict cannot publish a world.
- A completed Community Site opens the community research telescope launch for all
  agencies, including non-contributors. Ordinary launch, rocket and feed rules apply.
  ORI-1 and LEO-1 instead follow the actual shower start dates, as Liam requested;
  the shower run remains necessary for the event badge chain.
- The unrelated WATCH rail button and new 12-second survey scans are removed from
  the Data hub. Survey work opens real probe readings, or Program when a probe is
  needed. Already running legacy scans may finish. Payload flights do not warn about
  having no ore silo and fixed satellite destinations are already chosen in review.

The initial agency and servicing work was recorded on an unmerged branch. It is
integrated here with the current cycle's telescope upgrades and weekly passport.

Native receives the launch stamp, preserves the newer stamp kinds and unlocks its
Agency path. Full fleet/service/shared-snapshot presentation belongs to the five-minute
companion scope in SSL-869 under SSL-574. It was not built or run in this Linux workspace.
Automatic asteroid-world creation and using Saturn/radio findings to change world
weather remain world-generation expansion work; this closeout uses verified TESS worlds.
