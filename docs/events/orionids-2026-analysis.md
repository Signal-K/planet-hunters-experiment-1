# Orionids 2026: what the player actually does

Written 19 Oct 2026, after the peak-night debris boost, the all-day Base chip, the Spacetober strip, and the Orionids-found voucher. This is a description of the live web loop, not a pitch.

The shower is open from local midnight on 19 Oct through 8 Nov 2026 00:00 UTC. Debris only falls from 22:00 to 06:00 local. The advertised peak boost is local 21 Oct 18:00 until 22 Oct 06:00, and it doubles whatever the ramp is already producing. Outside that night the rate still moves: it starts at 4 particles a minute and climbs toward 12 as the UTC peak (22 Oct 00:00) gets closer, then falls back. Peak night multiplies that ramp by 2. A normal night in the window is the same scene at the un-doubled rate.

## A normal night in the window

Afternoon, any day from 19 Oct to 7 Nov. The player is on the base. A chip in the corner reads Orionids tonight, including at noon. Opening it shows the panel: Halley's Comet dust, the 22:00–06:00 window, three steps (fly a laser mining run at night, wait for debris, sell it at the Exchange), the badge line, Share, and View event. Nothing in the sky is different. The chip does not say how long until 22:00.

View event opens the Events board. Spacetober sits at the top: World Space Week first (4–10 Oct, already ended), then the Orionids peak (21 Oct 18:00–22 Oct 06:00). The week step shows a gold or silver set badge only if the player already earned a World Space Week badge. The peak step shows one only after an Orionids badge or an Orionids-found planet. Links go to Atlas badges, Garden, and the Atlas home page, which is the public check-in. youratlas.cc does not publish a separate Orionids URL. Under that, the old cards are still there: Orionids as a live event, and the four World Space Week cards as past events with Play for silver.

Participate on the Orionids card opens a Free run headed "Orionids night run". The contract is 8 iron. It is not a debris contract. Start run flies the same laser mining mission as any other free iron run. If the player does this before 22:00, the field has no debris. The run can be finished, the iron sold or stored, and the Orionids badge does not move.

After 22:00 the same run shows blue-white specks falling through the asteroid field while the laser mines rock. The first speck mined grants the Orionids badge, gold during the window. More specks are just cargo of the mineral Orionid debris, uncommon, sold at the Exchange at the ordinary spot price. There is no counter of specks, no comparison with last night, and no moment in the scene for moonset. When the run ends, the debrief is the normal mining debrief plus the badge row if a badge was earned.

If the player classifies a TESS candidate as a planet, or finishes a survey scan of a planet, during the window, that body gets an Orionids-found marker and an owned voucher. The toast says the voucher is owned and that redeem is coming soon. The Events strip lists it the same way. Meteorite mastery becomes 1, once. A second planet does not raise it. The training candidate does not count. An asteroid classification does not count. Before 19 Oct none of this is granted. After 8 Nov the same actions still grant the marker and the voucher, at silver, and still set mastery to 1 if it was 0.

## Peak night, 21 Oct 18:00 to 22 Oct 06:00

The clock is the only thing that makes this night different, and the game does not show that clock as a peak.

From 18:00 to 22:00 the boost is already on in the sim, but debris is still gated to local night, so the field is empty until 22:00. The Base chip still says Orionids tonight. The panel does say "Peak night 21–22 Oct: debris falls twice as fast." A player who reads the panel knows. A player who only looks at the field does not, until 22:00, and even then only if they remember a slower night.

At 22:00 the specks start. They come faster than on the 19th or the 20th. There is no new target, no new sound, no peak banner on the mining screen, and no change to the 8 iron goal. The badge, if it was not already earned on an earlier night, drops on the first speck exactly as before. Extra specks are extra cargo. The Exchange price does not rise because it is peak night.

At 02:00 local on 22 Oct one reminder fires, if the player allowed notifications: "Moon's down — look up. Orionids peak; debris is falling in Landnam." That is the only line in the product that mentions the real sky at the moment the real shower is best. The mining scene does not change at 02:00. The rate was already doubled from 18:00, and the particles were already falling from 22:00.

At 06:00 the specks stop. The chip goes back to the daytime "tonight" line. Cargo already collected is still in the hold or the silo. There is nothing else to finish.

## Is the goal clear?

The panel's three steps are clear: fly at night, laser the specks, sell them. The Free run's goal is a different sentence: bring back 8 iron. Both are on screen, and they are not the same goal. A player can complete the run and miss the shower, or mine debris and ignore the iron, and the game treats both as success of a sort. The badge is for the first speck, not for the iron and not for staying until dawn.

The new planet voucher is clearer than the shower, and smaller. Classifying a planet during the window produces a named marker and the words "Redeem coming soon." That is an honest label. It is not a goal the Events board asks for. Nothing on the Orionids card says "classify a planet."

## Are the choices meaningful?

Night versus day is a real choice: day has no debris. Which asteroid to mine is the same choice as a normal free run. Selling debris versus holding it is the normal Exchange choice. Peak night versus any other night is not a choice the board offers; it is a date. Classifying a planet as a planet, versus unsure, changes whether the marker is granted. That is meaningful only if the player already came to classify. The game does not ask them to do it for the shower.

There is no loadout, no site, and no timing decision that makes peak night play differently from 20 Oct at 23:00 except the number of specks.

## Is the feedback readable?

The chip, the panel, the night-run header, the badge, and the voucher line are readable. Body text on those surfaces is at least 14px, and the main buttons meet 44px. The thing that is not readable is the rate. Twice as fast is a sentence in a panel, not a number on the field. A player who has not mined on an earlier night has nothing to compare. Meteorite mastery 1 has no other level and no place it changes a tool. The voucher cannot be spent. The feedback for both is a label that says they exist.

## Is there a reason to come back?

One badge, then more debris to sell. After the first speck, the next night is the same run with a different spawn rate. The voucher does not bring anyone back, because redeem is not built. Mastery does not bring anyone back, because it does not change. World Space Week is over; its cards still offer silver for players who missed the week, which is a reason to open those four events, not a reason to stay up for the Orionids. The 02:00 reminder is a reason to look at the real sky. It is not a new thing to do in the game at 02:00.

## What is still missing for this to feel like an event

The shower is a stat bump on laser mining: more particles per minute, one badge, a mineral that sells like any other uncommon ore. Spacetober puts the week and the peak in order and stores a two-badge set, which is bookkeeping. The planet marker and the voucher are a second, quieter track that does not touch the mining night.

An event night would need a goal that is the shower, a readable difference on peak night, and a reason to be there at 02:00 rather than at 22:30. None of those are in this build. The copy that comes closest is the reminder, and it points outside the game.
