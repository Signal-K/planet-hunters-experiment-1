Minimal scenery sketches

Three new looks for the pictures behind Minimal's screens: home, the launch, and a destination. Switch the view below; all three frames follow it. Tap a frame to launch its rocket. Rough sketches, not final art.

Why today's view is hard to follow

  • The ground is a quilt. Every tile is shaded on its own and every one-voxel step draws an edge, so flat steppe reads as a patchwork of light and dark diamonds. There is no single shape for the eye to land on.
  • The subject is small. The outpost takes about 1/5 of the frame; the rest is terrain that means nothing. Its buildings are one tile each and read as marks (two blue squares, a yellow cage), not as a base.
  • There is no up. A 40° camera on an upright phone looks at ground from top to bottom: no horizon, no sky. A launch has nowhere to go, so the rocket leaves into more ground.
  • Hard biome edges (the green block) and a drifting camera with no landmark add movement without meaning.

A · Horizon slice

Recommended
Side-on · sky above, ground cut open below

Look at the place from the side, like a cut through the ground. The tall side of the phone becomes sky, so the rocket climbs the whole screen. The base stands on one clear skyline, big enough to read each building. Below the surface you see the ground in section, so at a destination the deposit is visible in the rock: the same picture the scan reveals in the mining pass.

Can come straight from TakeOn's world: the skyline is one row of the real heightfield and the section is its real voxel columns, ore included. Loses the "walk around a world" feeling, which Minimal does not use anyway.

B · Low camera

Ground-level 3D · horizon at the top third

Drop the camera to just above the ground and look across it to a horizon. Terrain is smooth rolling ground that fades into the sky, with no tiles. The base sits big in the foreground and the rocket rises into open sky.

Feels most like standing on a world. TakeOn has no 3D view, so this is a new renderer (a real perspective camera, likely WebGL), the most work of the three, and deposits are hard to show from this angle.

C · Island diorama

Today's isometric view · one small floating island

Keep TakeOn's isometric engine, but show only a small cut-out of the world, about 7 by 7 tiles, floating in sky. Calm ground with two shades, not a quilt. Bigger buildings that fill the island. The island's cut edge shows the strata, and the ore seam at a destination.

Smallest change to TakeOn: a crop, a calmer ground palette and bigger props. Still a toy-like view seen from above, and the rocket still has only the sky around the island to climb.

What I'd do

  1. Build A as a new view in TakeOn's minimal scene (KES-530): a side renderer that reads the same generated world, so every body keeps its real ground, colours and ore. Minimal's home, launch, route and destination screens all switch to it.
  2. Keep the isometric view for the full TakeOn rover game; only Minimal's pictures change.
  3. If you want the world to feel walkable instead, B, accepting a new 3D renderer. C only if we need something this week.
